# The reader drops anything it cannot be sure of rather than guess, which is
# the right way round: a hotspot on the wrong part sends a technician to order
# the wrong thing, and no hotspot only sends them to the list underneath. So
# these four are what is left after it has been careful, not what it got wrong.

# Fig.1 — both are clearly printed two-digit callouts that the shape filters
# turned down. 28 sits directly above the bearing it points at, its leader
# running back into the digits; 31 stands between the washer and the bearing
# with a long leader crossing other line work.
1 add 28 67.1 37.0
1 add 31 69.4 41.1

# Fig.3 — a lone "1" at the far left edge, with its leader running away to the
# boxed metering assembly, and the box's own dash-dot border a few millimetres
# to its right. A single vertical stroke beside a dashed line is exactly what
# the reader refuses to call a digit, and it is right to: on a page of line art
# that rule is what keeps false "1"s off every other drawing.
3 add 1 4.6 54.5

# NOT PLACED, DELIBERATELY: Fig.2 key 11, "3407-16120 Screw".
#
# It is not printed on the drawing. What IS printed, pointing at the little
# screw beside clamp 10, is a single "1" - checked at high magnification, one
# glyph with clear white paper either side of it, at 67.9% / 38.7%. The volute
# case, which is key 1, is already called out at the top right pointing at the
# housing itself, so the sheet carries "1" twice and no "11" anywhere.
#
# A screw drawn next to a clamp, in a list that runs 10 Clamp, 11 Screw, 12
# Tube, is almost certainly meant to be 11 - but "almost certainly" is how a
# technician ends up ordering the wrong screw, and the app would be asserting
# it rather than the book. The part is still in the list under the drawing.
